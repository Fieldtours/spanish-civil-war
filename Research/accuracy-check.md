# The Spanish Civil War — facts for JC to check

Written 27 September 2026. Everything below is also marked in the app where a visitor needs it (the dashed "Where sources disagree" boxes). Please read sections 1 and 2; the rest is for reference.

## 1. Big disputed numbers and questions (shown to visitors as ranges)

- **Total deaths**: the "about half a million" figure is a common one, but estimates vary widely. The JSON says so rather than giving one number.
- **Repression**: for the Nationalist zone during the war, estimates run from under 50,000 to 200,000. For the Republican zone they run from 38,000 to 55,000 among mainstream historians, and up to 110,965 in outlier estimates. Both ranges are given with the authors' names.
- **International Brigades**: the text uses "commonly 32,000–35,000". Wikipedia's infobox gives 59,380, which is higher than most historians' figures.
- **Condor Legion and CTV**: "about 16,000 Germans over time" and "up to about 50,000 Italians at a time" come from the Wikipedia infobox. Other figures exist.
- **Gernika**: the Basque government gave 1,654 dead in 1937. Modern research gives about 126–300.
- **Badajoz**: estimates run from 500–700 to about 4,000. Espinosa documented a minimum of 1,341.
- **Paracuellos**: the death toll (commonly 2,000–3,000, with claims far higher) and who was responsible (Carrillo, the Junta, Soviet advisers) are both disputed.
- **Desbandá**: about 3,000 deaths is often cited, and some modern historians think it was lower.
- **Durango**: the text says "some 250 or more". This was not checked against a primary source.
- **Durruti's death**: how he died is disputed. **Capa's "Falling Soldier"**: whether it was staged, and where it was taken, are disputed. **The Moscardó phone call**: the details are questioned.
- **1936 election**: the fairness of the count in some provinces is disputed (Villa García and Álvarez Tardío, 2017, and their critics).
- **Clock times** marked MEDIUM (Queipo c. 13:15, Dragon Rapide c. 14:30, Barcelona c. 04:00, Tetuán 07:30) come from Spanish Wikipedia. Other accounts differ by up to an hour or so. Franco's manifesto time was not checked.
- **Aragon offensive start date**: given as 7 March 1938. Some sources say 9 March.

## 2. Where each site's story says historians disagree

- **No. 1 Ciudad Universitaria front** — How Durruti was shot (enemy sniper, accidental discharge, or killed by his own side) remains debated.
- **No. 2 Alcázar of Toledo & Army Museum** — The famous telephone call in which Moscardó told his son Luis to 'die like a hero' is questioned by historians: some say Luis was shot about a month later, in reprisal for an air raid, not in response to the call. The fate of the hostages inside is also unclear.
- **No. 3 Suicide Hill (Colina del Suicidio), Jarama** — Casualty figures for the British Battalion vary between sources (commonly 375 of about 600, or around 125 left unhurt of 400).
- **No. 4 Pingarrón hill, Jarama** — Lincoln Battalion losses on 27 Feb are given variously (e.g. 120 dead and 175 wounded).
- **No. 5 Valle de Cuelgamuros (former Valle de los Caídos)** — Numbers vary: around 33,800 people are recorded as interred; how many prisoners worked on the building is disputed (official records of a few hundred convicts at a time, against estimates of up to 20,000 over the whole project).
- **No. 6 Paracuellos de Jarama cemetery** — Death toll: modern historians mostly give 2,000–3,000 (Thomas c.2,000; Casanova c.2,700), against older claims up to 12,000. Carrillo's personal responsibility is the subject of continuing debate.
- **No. 12 Templo de Debod — site of the Cuartel de la Montaña** — The number of defenders killed after the surrender is uncertain; accounts range from dozens to several hundred.
- **No. 16 Brunete** — Republican losses are given as anything from 10,000 to 25,000 killed and wounded; Nationalist losses about 7,000–17,000 depending on source.
- **No. 17 Brihuega & Palacio de Ibarra** — Italian losses are variously given as 600–3,000 dead and 2,000–4,000 wounded; the Republic's propaganda magnified the victory.
- **No. 20 Cementerio Civil & Almudena execution wall** — The memorial with the names of the executed commissioned in 2018 was altered by the city council in 2019 after disputes over errors in the lists; the number of those executed (c. 2,900–3,000) is based on cemetery records.
- **No. 31 Belchite old town (Pueblo Viejo)** — Casualty and garrison figures vary between sources. The numbers here follow English Wikipedia and cover only the town, not the whole offensive.
- **No. 34 Pozos de Caudé memorial (Concud)** — Victim numbers are not established and estimates vary greatly. The TracesOfWar entry says killings were committed by both sides as Teruel changed hands, but most sources attribute the well killings to the Nationalist rearguard.
- **No. 38 Quinto – Purburel / Cabezo de la Nariz trenches** — The garrison figures are estimates from the local visit guide, with wide margins (±150).
- **No. 39 Torrero Cemetery memorials (Zaragoza)** — The total number of people executed at Torrero is still researched and debated. About 2,500 refers only to the remains found in 1979.
- **No. 40 Basílica del Pilar – the unexploded bombs (Zaragoza)** — Wikipedia says only that the bombs fell during the war.
- **No. 41 Alcañiz air-raid shelter** — The death toll is uncertain: sources give between about 300 and more than 500 dead and missing.
- **No. 43 Codo – battle of the Montserrat requetés** — Casualty numbers come from pro-requeté sources.
- **No. 46 Montearagón castle (Huesca siege lines)** — The castle's own site gives no Civil War details. Its role is taken from the Aragon heritage entry for Estrecho Quinto.
- **No. 48 Bielsa museum – the Bielsa Pocket** — The dates and unit follow standard accounts because the museum's own page could not be read.
- **No. 54 Muela de Villaspesa** — TracesOfWar does not say which side held the Muela. The layout suggests the Nationalists, but this is not confirmed.
- **No. 57 Museu Memorial de la Batalla de l'Ebre (CEBE), Gandesa** — Casualty totals for the battle vary widely: Preston gives about 7,150 Republican and 6,100 Nationalist dead, Thomas 10,000–15,000 Republican and 6,500 Nationalist dead; total casualty estimates run from 50,000–60,000 to over 100,000.
- **No. 58 Refugi 307, Poble-sec (MUHBA)** — Death tolls for the March 1938 raids are usually given as 1,000–1,300 killed, with at least 2,000 wounded.
- **No. 59 Fossar de la Pedrera, Montjuïc** — Figures differ: about 1,717 executions in Barcelona are documented for 1939–52 (plaques give 1,734 for Camp de la Bóta), while some sources give around 4,000 burials in the grave, including bombing victims.
- **No. 62 MUME – Museu Memorial de l'Exili, La Jonquera** — Estimates of the number who crossed into France in early 1939 range from about 450,000 to 500,000.
- **No. 66 Vilalba dels Arcs: Els Barrancs trenches & Mirador del Bassot** — Sources are unclear on which army built the Barrancs trenches.
- **No. 71 Telefónica building & Plaça de Catalunya (May Days 1937)** — Deaths in the May Days: contemporary press reported about 500 killed; recent research puts it at about 218–235 in Barcelona.
- **No. 75 Camp de la Bóta memorial (Fraternitat)** — The plaque gives 1,734 people shot; other counts give 1,717 executions in Barcelona for 1939–52. The number of Republican-period executions here in 1936 is not well documented.
- **No. 77 Barcelona Cathedral – memorial to clergy killed in 1936** — Nationwide, about 6,800 clergy were killed in the Republican zone (A. Montero's count).
- **No. 80 La Vajol & Coll de Lli – exile crossing** — Details of the route taken by each leader vary between accounts.
- **No. 84 Gernika Peace Museum (Museo de la Paz)** — Death toll: the Basque Government reported 1,645 dead in 1937; most modern historians put it between about 126 and 300; Xabier Irujo (2017) argues for a much higher figure. Records were destroyed and both sides used the numbers for propaganda.
- **No. 87 Durango bombing – Santa María de Uribarri** — Deaths are given as about 250 in most accounts; Spanish-language studies give a range of 248–336.
- **No. 89 Santander air-raid shelter (Refugio Antiaéreo)** — The tourist office says Santander suffered 34 air raids; other counts differ.
- **No. 90 El Mazuco & Sierra del Cuera** — Sources give different lengths for the battle, from about 17 days (5/6–22 Sep) to '33 days of combat'.
- **No. 94 Otxandio – Andikona bombing memorial** — Deaths are recorded as 57–61; Paul Preston cites 84. There is debate over whether this or the 18 July raid on Tetuán was the war's first bombing.
- **No. 96 Puerto del Escudo – Italian memorial** — This is a Fascist-era monument; its future is debated under Spain's democratic memory legislation.
- **No. 97 Oviedo siege – memorial to the defenders** — Casualties: about 2,500 among the defenders and at least 5,000 among the attacking militias (Wikipedia); civilian deaths are unclear.
- **No. 98 El Salvador cemetery mass grave, Oviedo** — The overall death toll of post-war repression in Asturias is still being researched; 1,316 is the number of identified names on the memorial.
- **No. 99 Monte Sollube battlefield** — Casualty figures (about 400 Nationalist and 600 Republican dead) come from a single summary source.
- **No. 101 Simancas barracks siege memorial, Gijón** — This is a Francoist commemorative monument; its current condition and status under democratic memory law have not been checked.
- **No. 105 Refugios de la Guerra Civil, Almería (air-raid shelters)** — Capacity and number of raids vary between sources (40,000 people is the usual figure; the memorial association says over 75% of the population). Deaths in the 31 May 1937 bombardment are given as 19–20.
- **No. 106 Barranco de Víznar (mass graves, Place of Memory)** — The total number of people killed and buried at Víznar–Alfacar is unknown; estimates run from hundreds to over a thousand. Exhumation counts rise with each campaign.
- **No. 107 Parque Federico García Lorca, Alfacar (Fuente Grande)** — The date of Lorca's death is given as 17, 18 or 19 August 1936, and the exact site and his grave are unknown; the 2009 dig at the olive tree found nothing.
- **No. 109 Paseo de los Canadienses / Peñón del Cuervo — 'La Desbandá' memorial, Málaga** — Death toll unknown. Figures of 3,000–5,000 dead (some claims far higher) are widely quoted; historians such as Antonio Nadal think the true figure may be significantly lower. Estimates of the number fleeing run from about 100,000 to nearly 300,000 (Junta de Andalucía).
- **No. 110 Santuario de la Virgen de la Cabeza (1936–37 siege), Andújar** — The monument and its inscriptions reflect the Francoist narrative and are debated under Spain's democratic memory laws. Numbers of besiegers are given as 6,000–12,000.
- **No. 111 Lopera — battlefield and Jardín de los Poetas Ingleses (Cornford & Fox)** — Losses vary widely: about 800 Brigade casualties incl. 300 dead (Wikipedia) vs about 500 casualties (Junta). Of the British/Irish company, 78 of 145 are said to have been lost; the IBMT counts 13 British and Irish dead. Fox's death is dated 27 or 28 December.
- **No. 112 Parque de la Memoria 'Málaga 1937', Torre del Mar** — Death toll of the Desbandá is disputed (see Paseo de los Canadienses entry).
- **No. 113 Panteón de la Memoria, Cementerio de San Rafael, Málaga** — Total victims of the Nationalist repression in Málaga are variously estimated at around 4,000 or more; killings in the Republican zone in Málaga (July 1936–Feb 1937) are estimated at around 2,500. Figures vary by historian.
- **No. 114 Cementerio de San Fernando — Pico Reja mass grave, Seville** — Number of dead in Pico Reja and of Seville's repression overall is disputed; identification of Infante's remains had not been confirmed at time of writing.
- **No. 116 Espejo — site of Capa's 'Falling Soldier'** — Location (Cerro Muriano vs Espejo), identity of the soldier and whether the scene was staged are all disputed.
- **No. 117 Puente Viejo de Maro (Desbandá crossing), Nerja** — Numbers killed on this stretch are unknown.
- **No. 118 Puerto de Almería & Parque de las Almadrabillas** — Numbers of Andalusian deportees to Mauthausen vary between sources.
- **No. 119 Fuerte de Carchuna and the Motril front, Granada coast** — Number freed is given as around 300; 308 per the La Desbandá association.
- **No. 120 Tablada airfield (Army of Africa airlift), Seville** — Numbers flown across are given differently in sources.
- **No. 121 Refugio-Museo de la Guerra Civil, Cartagena** — The number of raids on Cartagena is given as anywhere from 40 to 117, depending on the source and on how raids are counted.
- **No. 122 Refugio antiaéreo del Ayuntamiento, Valencia** — Totals for the bombing of Valencia vary. Tourism Valencia gives more than 440 raids, about 800 dead and 3,000 wounded; other counts differ.
- **No. 123 Mercado Central, Alicante (bombing of 25 May 1938)** — Death toll: the Republican war report gives 250, cemetery records about 275, the municipal plaque 311, and a Francoist document 393. More than 1,000 people were wounded.
- **No. 124 Port of Alicante – Stanbrook and the last refugees** — Stanbrook passengers are given as 2,638 by some sources and 'about 3,000' by others. Estimates of those left behind range from 15,000 to more than 20,000.
- **No. 125 Línea XYZ – La Muela & La Vegatilla trenches, Jérica** — Beevor's view that the defence was a greater Republican victory than Guadalajara is an interpretation.
- **No. 128 Campo de Los Almendros memorial, Alicante** — Prisoner numbers range from 18,000 to 30,000, and deaths are estimated at anywhere from 600 to 2,000; there are no reliable records.
- **No. 131 Albatera concentration camp memorial, San Isidro** — The number of prisoners in 1939 is uncertain because the records were destroyed; estimates range from about 7,000 to 30,000.
- **No. 132 Batería de Castillitos, Cabo Tiñoso (Cartagena)** — The claim that Castillitos fired only once, in 1937, comes from secondary tourism sources.
- **No. 136 Es Campament penal colony, La Savina (Formentera)** — Sources give 1,400 as the usual capacity and 2,133 as the total number who passed through; do not confuse the two figures.
- **No. 137 Refugio de la calle Serranos, Valencia** — It is very probably, but not certainly, the Calle Serranos 25 shelter.
- **No. 138 Puerta de la Trinidad, Badajoz** — Legion losses at the gate vary by source (from about 20 dead and 22 wounded to 76 of about 90 men of the 16th Company).
- **No. 139 Former bullring (Palacio de Congresos) – Badajoz massacre memorial** — Death toll strongly disputed: contemporary press reported 1,800–4,000 killed in the city; later historians (e.g. Francisco Espinosa) give several thousand victims in the city over the following months, while some revisionist authors argue for a few hundred. Whether mass shootings took place inside the bullring itself is also debated by the revisionist side.
- **No. 140 Castuera concentration camp (La Verilleja)** — Prisoner numbers range from 8,000–9,000 (Julián Chaves) to 15,000–20,000 in other estimates; the number executed is not established.
- **No. 141 Puente Romano, Mérida (fall of Mérida)** — Casualties of the fighting are not reliably known; numbers for the subsequent repression in Mérida vary between studies.
- **No. 143 Almendralejo – Purificación church tower & memorial** — Numbers killed after the capture vary widely: some accounts give 28 on entry, Portuguese journalists reported up to 1,000; the 2023 memorial names 714 victims (town + other localities). The number of right-wing victims killed earlier in the town is also debated.
- **No. 147 Centro Documental de la Memoria Histórica, Salamanca** — The return of the 'Salamanca papers' to Catalonia was politically contested in Salamanca and in court.
- **No. 148 Monumento a los Caídos, Pamplona** — Its future (resignification vs. demolition) remains contested among city parties and memory associations.
- **No. 151 Parque de la Memoria, Sartaguda** — Total figures for Navarre's repression (commonly over 3,000) come from memory-association research; exact numbers vary.
- **No. 152 Miranda de Ebro concentration camp** — Total prisoner numbers are estimates.
- **No. 154 Isla de San Simón (concentration camp), Ría de Vigo** — Total deaths are estimates.

## 7. How the pins were checked

All 158 pins were checked by computer against OpenStreetMap (to confirm each one sits in the right town) and against Wikipedia coordinates where an article exists. 10 pins were corrected (section 3). HIGH means a museum, memorial, ruin or trench with a published position.
