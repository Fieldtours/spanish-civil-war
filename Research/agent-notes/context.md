## Doubts and disputed points (marked in the text too)
- **Total deaths**: the "about half a million" figure is a common one, but estimates vary widely. The JSON says so rather than giving one number.
- **Repression**: for the Nationalist zone during the war, estimates run from under 50,000 to 200,000. For the Republican zone they run from 38,000 to 55,000 among mainstream historians, and up to 110,965 in outlier estimates. Both ranges are given with the authors' names.
- **International Brigades**: the text uses "commonly 32,000–35,000". Wikipedia's infobox gives 59,380, which is higher than most historians' figures.
- **Condor Legion and CTV**: "about 16,000 Germans over time" and "up to about 50,000 Italians at a time" come from the Wikipedia infobox. Other figures exist.
- **Gernika**: the Basque government gave 1,654 dead in 1937. Modern research gives about 126–300.
- **Badajoz**: estimates run from 500–700 to about 4,000. Espinosa documented a minimum of 1,341.
- **Paracuellos**: the death toll (commonly 2,000–3,000, with claims far higher) and who was responsible (Carrillo, the Junta, Soviet advisers) are both disputed.
- **Desbandá**: about 3,000 deaths is often cited, and some modern historians think it was lower.
- **Durango**: the text says "some 250 or more". This was not checked against a primary source.
- **Durruti's death**: how he died is disputed. **Capa's "Falling Soldier"**: whether it was staged, and where it was taken, are disputed. **The Moscardó phone call**: the details are questioned.
- **1936 election**: the fairness of the count in some provinces is disputed (Villa García and Álvarez Tardío, 2017, and their critics).
- **Clock times** marked MEDIUM (Queipo c. 13:15, Dragon Rapide c. 14:30, Barcelona c. 04:00, Tetuán 07:30) come from Spanish Wikipedia. Other accounts differ by up to an hour or so. Franco's manifesto time was not checked.
- **Coordinates**:
  - City and town centres and major sites are standard.
  - Coordinates rated LOW or MEDIUM: Alcocero de Mola (approximate, LOW), Alicante prison (city, LOW), Palacio de la Isla in Burgos (approximate, MEDIUM), Tablada airfield, Serra de Pàndols, the Víznar–Alfacar ravine, the Flix/Riba-roja crossing and the Diagonal parade point.
  - The Paracuellos cemetery (40.5117, -3.5469) was converted from the DMS in the Wikipedia article.
- **Aragon offensive start date**: given as 7 March 1938. Some sources say 9 March.
- **Battle maps**: these are schematic and only roughly geographic. Unit placements and phase details are simplified from the Wikipedia battle articles. The `stop` field is left empty because stop ids from the other agents were not known.
- **Links**: the Valencia MHV link is the museum's home page, which advertises shelter visits. The Mallorca link is a Balearic government news item, since no dedicated tourism page was found. The Extremadura links are a local museum and a comarca tourism site, not regional government pages; the search budget ran out before one could be found.
